The lab is a single domain, `lab.local`, with one domain controller at `10.10.10.5` and two member servers. It runs on my machine, offline.

## Step 1 — Knock, knock

<CodeBlock lang="bash" host="attacker" lines={["$ nmap -sV -sC -p 53,88,135,389,445 10.10.10.5"]} />

Ports 88 (Kerberos) and 389 (LDAP) confirm a domain controller.

## Step 2 — Find accounts with SPNs

With a low-privilege lab account, list service accounts. See the [Kerberoasting video](/videos/kerberoasting/) for the full walkthrough.

<CodeBlock lang="bash" host="attacker" lines={["$ impacket-GetUserSPNs lab.local/<USER>:<PASSWORD> -dc-ip 10.10.10.5"]} />

## Step 3 — Crack the service ticket

The `svc_sql` password was in the wordlist. Service accounts with human-chosen passwords are the weak link.

## Step 4 — Move to the SQL server

`svc_sql` is a local administrator on `sql01`. A network logon there gives a shell as that account.

## Step 5 — Domain admin

A domain admin had an interactive session on `sql01`, so their credentials were in memory. That is the end of the domain.

## How each step looks in the logs

| Step | Evidence | Event ID |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Many connections from one host to the DC | Firewall logs |
| 2 and 3 | Service ticket requests with RC4 encryption | 4769 |
| 4 | Network logon as `svc_sql` on `sql01` | 4624 (type 3) |
| 5 | Logon with a domain admin account from `sql01` | 4624, 4672 |

<Callout type="defence" title="what would have stopped this">
  - gMSA for `svc_sql`, so there is no crackable password.
  - Service accounts are not local admins anywhere they do not need to be.
  - Domain admins never log on to member servers. Use a tiered admin model.
</Callout>
